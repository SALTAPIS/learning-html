A little history

A box with a single switch. Flip it on, and the lid opens, a hand reaches out, switches it off again and disappears. That is all it does.

The idea came from Marvin Minsky in the early 1950s, while he was a graduate student at Bell Telephone Laboratories. His colleague Claude Shannon, the father of information theory, built a version and kept it on his desk, where it became known as the Ultimate Machine.

Science-fiction writer Arthur C. Clarke saw it at Shannon's office and later wrote that there was "something unspeakably sinister about a machine that does nothing — absolutely nothing — except switch itself off."
